# PracticeSpend — Product Brief

## Purpose

PracticeSpend is a lightweight, independent purchasing-intelligence product for medical practices. It does not replace a purchasing system, accounting system, EHR, bill-pay platform, credit card, vendor marketplace, or inventory system.

Its job is simpler:

> Analyze historical purchasing evidence and tell the practice what it should know or investigate.

Invoices are the initial evidence source. They are not the product boundary.

## Initial target user

Independent and physician-owned specialty practices, beginning with dermatology and aesthetic dermatology.

The prototype is specifically intended to be credible to a practice with the complexity of Modern Dermatology in Seattle: medical, surgical, aesthetic, injectables, devices/consumables, and retail skincare.

## Core pain hypothesis

Independent practices often lack dedicated procurement and financial-analysis teams. Purchasing information may be spread across invoices, vendor portals, accounting systems, spreadsheets, emailed quotes, and staff memory.

This can make it difficult to answer basic questions such as:

- What did we actually pay for this item over time?
- Did this vendor quietly raise the price?
- Are we buying the same item at materially different prices?
- Are shipping, handling, and order fragmentation creating avoidable expense?
- Are invoiced prices inconsistent with negotiated or contracted prices?
- Which suppliers and products account for most of our spend?
- Which apparent savings opportunities are verified versus merely possible?

## Product thesis

PracticeSpend wins by being an independent second opinion on purchasing.

It should not require the practice to:

- change vendors;
- route purchases through PracticeSpend;
- replace its EHR or accounting system;
- connect a bank account;
- issue a new credit card;
- permit PracticeSpend to place orders;
- upload patient records;
- migrate its purchasing workflow.

The user can simply provide purchasing history and ask: **What happened to our money?**

## Competitive thesis

PracticeSpend should not attempt to outbuild enterprise procurement, AP automation, or inventory platforms.

Its differentiators are:

1. **Independent** — not tied to a marketplace, distributor, card, or purchasing channel.
2. **Read-only first** — it analyzes evidence without needing authority to spend.
3. **Historical** — it can analyze what has already happened, including old invoices.
4. **System-agnostic** — CSV/XLSX/manual data can originate from any vendor or system.
5. **Explainable** — every finding must trace back to underlying transactions and math.
6. **Low friction** — no implementation project is required for the prototype.
7. **Local-first prototype** — uploaded data can remain entirely in the user's browser.

## Prototype success condition

The prototype succeeds if James Collyer and Heather Rogers can use it for five minutes and answer:

1. I understand what this does.
2. This looks like a real product, not a mockup.
3. This addresses a real operational or financial pain in a practice like ours.
4. I would be willing to test this with our own non-PHI purchasing information.
5. I can imagine another practice paying for this.

## Product boundary

PracticeSpend does **not** diagnose, treat, recommend clinical care, process patient information, or make clinical decisions.

The prototype is for business purchasing information only.
